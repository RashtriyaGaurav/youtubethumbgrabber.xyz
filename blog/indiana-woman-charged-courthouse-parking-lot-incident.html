Woman Arrested for Running Over Ex-Husband Outside Courthouse Following Divorce Proceedings

A divorce hearing in Crown Point, Indiana ended with a felony arrest after a parking-lot confrontation between two spouses.

Reporting via Law&Crime and CBS Chicago, based on a probable cause affidavit and statements from Lake County authorities. Charges are allegations; the case has not gone to trial.

A 32-year-old Crown Point woman, Adriana Cisneros Velazquez, is facing an attempted murder charge and several felony battery counts following an incident outside the Lake County Government Center on August 24. According to a probable cause affidavit obtained by Law&Crime, the confrontation happened shortly after Velazquez and her husband left a divorce hearing at the courthouse.

What was reported

A witness told investigators he saw Velazquez driving erratically in a gray Honda before she allegedly accelerated toward her husband, who was on a motorcycle in the parking lot. Police say he was struck and briefly dragged before freeing himself. Velazquez's car reportedly became immobilized after the collision, and the affidavit states she then got out with a knife; the man was treated at a hospital for a stab wound and other injuries from the incident.

Lake County Sheriff Oscar Martinez Jr. told CBS Chicago that both people were taken to a hospital for evaluation. Prosecutors filed five felony counts on August 25, including Level 1 felony attempted murder, two attempted aggravated battery counts, and two counts of domestic battery with a deadly weapon, according to court records cited by Law&Crime and Region News Source.

Where the case stands

Velazquez was advised of her rights and declined to give a statement to detectives, per the reporting. She remains held at the Lake County Jail without bond as of this writing, with the case now moving through the Lake County court system. As with any pending criminal case, the charges represent allegations only — Velazquez has not been convicted, and the facts described above come from police affidavits and preliminary reporting rather than a court finding.
